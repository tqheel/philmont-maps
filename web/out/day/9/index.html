Overview

Bear Caves is a trail camp in the southwest country, positioned near the Crater Lake staffed camp area where the day's programs occur. The name refers to actual rock overhangs and shallow caves in the area that have historically attracted bear activity — proper food storage is especially important here. The terrain transitions from the Black Mountain area toward the rolling country approaching Urraca Mesa.

Programs & What to Expect (at Crater Lake)

Continental Tie and Lumber Company Program: Crater Lake's program brings to life the early 20th-century logging operations of the Continental Tie and Lumber Company (CT&L), which operated in Philmont's canyon country in the 1900s–1910s. CT&L cut timber in the Ponil and Rayado drainages to produce railroad ties for the expanding rail network.

Activities include:

  • Spar pole climbing: Scouts use climbing spurs (gaffs) and a safety belt to ascend a tall wooden spar pole — a traditional logging skill. This is a physically demanding upper-body and grip challenge. No prior experience needed; staff teach the technique.
  • Crosscut saw: Two-person traditional saws used to cut logs — "misery whip" in logger slang. Crews work in pairs. Pure teamwork.
  • Hand-hewn railroad tie construction: Using a broadaxe, scouts learn to shape a round log into a flat-sided railroad tie.
  • Loggerball: A logger-themed variant of baseball, played in the evening

Gear/Clothing Notes

  • Long pants are recommended (and may be required) for spar pole climbing, which involves leg gaffs and a tree trunk; long pants protect against bark abrasion
  • Sturdy footwear (hiking boots) for spar pole work
  • No special gear beyond standard trail kit

Tips

  • Bear activity is historically noted in this area; hang food bags and dispose of waste properly
  • The spar pole climb is physically demanding on grip and arms; crew members with upper body strength concerns should still attempt it — staff will help them get as high as they can safely go
  • Crater Lake's view of the Tooth of Time is worth noting for the crew: that ridge is where they finish in 3 days
