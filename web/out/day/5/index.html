Overview

Day 5 involves a significant elevation gain — from Fish Camp (~7,040 ft) up to Buck Creek at over 9,100 ft. The route passes through Apache Springs (a staffed camp at approximately 9,400 ft), where the day's programs occur, before descending slightly to the Buck Creek trail camp for the night. This is one of the longer and more demanding hiking days on the itinerary.

Programs & What to Expect (at Apache Springs)

3D Archery — Jicarilla Apache Style: Apache Springs is home to Philmont's only backcountry archery course. The course uses 3D foam animal targets positioned throughout the terrain — deer, elk, bear, and other animals in realistic poses at varying distances. Equipment (bows, arrows, arm guards) is provided by the camp. No prior archery experience is required. Staff explain the cultural context of archery within Jicarilla Apache tradition.

Jicarilla Apache Life Program: Apache Springs is Philmont's primary interpretive camp for the Jicarilla Apache people who inhabited this region until the mid-19th century. The program includes:

  • Tipi tours (the camp features a village of historically accurate tipis with genuine artifacts)
  • Flint knapping — Scouts make real arrowheads using traditional stone-knapping techniques; this is a genuine skill and takes focused effort
  • Sweat lodge — a cultural and spiritual experience conducted respectfully
  • History talks on Jicarilla history, lifestyle, and displacement
  • Optional sunset meadow hike

Commissary: Apache Springs operates a full commissary with food resupply and white gas. It also has a small trading post where basic gear can be purchased. This is a good place to resupply any food that has been consumed faster than planned.

Gear/Clothing Notes

  • No special archery gear needed — all equipment is provided at Apache Springs
  • Closed-toed shoes are required for archery (already standard Philmont footwear)
  • The approach to Apache Springs involves significant climbing; trekking poles are useful
  • Buck Creek at 9,131 ft can be cold overnight — layers ready for camp

Tips

  • Apache Springs sits at the highest point of the day (9,400 ft); crew members may feel altitude effects even if acclimatized — keep the pace moderate on the climb
  • The flint knapping activity takes time; if the crew wants to complete it, do not rush through the other programs
  • Top off water at Apache Springs commissary before the descent to Buck Creek
  • Buck Creek is a trail camp with no staff; bears are active in this area — hang bags properly
  • The commissary sometimes stocks fresh fruit; take advantage of it
  • Advisor Coffee is available at Apache Springs.
