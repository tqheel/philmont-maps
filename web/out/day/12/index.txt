<h4 id="overview">Overview</h4>
<p>Day 12 is the emotional and physical finale of the trek. The crew climbs the Tooth of Time Ridge, summits the iconic Tooth (9,003 ft), and descends into Base Camp for the Awards Campfire. The Tooth of Time is Philmont&#39;s signature landmark — a dramatic rock spire visible from Base Camp and from miles around. Summiting it on the final day is a rite of passage.</p>
<p>The approach follows Tooth Ridge from Stockade Ridge, traversing a rocky ridgeline before the final Class 3 scramble to the summit.</p>
<h4 id="programs--what-to-expect">Programs &amp; What to Expect</h4>
<p><strong>Tooth Ridge Traverse:</strong>
From Stockade Ridge, the trail climbs through ponderosa forest and then onto rocky, open ridgeline. Once above tree line, the terrain is large boulders and exposed rock. The approach along the ridge is Class 2 — rocky, requiring attention to footing, but not technical. Some sections involve navigating over and around large boulders.</p>
<p>From Shaefer&#39;s Pass (a saddle on the ridge), the trail to the Tooth is approximately 3.5 miles total to the summit. The final quarter mile to the top is the Class 3 section.</p>
<p><strong>Tooth of Time Summit — Class 3 Scramble:</strong>
The final 440 feet of elevation gain to the summit is a genuine boulder scramble — Class 3 in the standard climbing classification, meaning hands and feet are used but no rope is needed. You will use your hands for balance and holds, particularly in the final approach. The scramble is manageable for any crew member who can hike Philmont; it is not technical climbing.</p>
<p><strong>Standard practice:</strong> crews drop their full packs at the base of the final scramble and summit with day packs or just water bottles. Do not attempt the final scramble with a full 35–45 lb pack.</p>
<p><strong>At the summit:</strong> 360-degree views of the Sangre de Cristo range, Cimarron valley, and surrounding Philmont terrain. The crew will receive a summit patch. This is a genuine high point of the BSA experience.</p>
<p><strong>Descent to Base Camp:</strong> After summiting, descend via the established trail back to Base Camp. The trail is steep in places; trekking poles are useful on the descent.</p>
<p><strong>Awards Campfire:</strong> Base Camp hosts an Awards Campfire for crews returning that day. Patches, recognitions, and program awards are distributed. The closing campfire marks the official end of the trek.</p>
<h4 id="gearclothing-notes">Gear/Clothing Notes</h4>
<p><strong>For the Tooth of Time Scramble:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Gloves:</strong> Optional but recommended. The boulder field in the final scramble involves rough granite surfaces; thin leather work gloves or light hiking gloves protect palms during sustained scrambling. Not required, but experienced hikers bring them.</li>
<li><strong>Sun protection is critical:</strong> the ridge is fully exposed with minimal shade. Sunscreen (reapply), sunglasses, and a hat are essential. The scramble section can be hot in late morning/early afternoon.</li>
<li><strong>Insect repellent:</strong> The summit in warm season attracts large numbers of ladybugs and gnats. Descend even 10 feet below the summit to escape them.</li>
<li><strong>Trekking poles:</strong> Useful on the approach and descent. Stow them during the Class 3 scramble itself (poles are a hazard during hand-and-foot scrambling).</li>
<li><strong>Rain awareness:</strong> The Tooth is fully exposed to weather. If thunderstorms are building, do not be on or near the summit after noon. Check the forecast on Day 11 and be prepared to start early on Day 12 to summit before afternoon storm development.</li>
<li><strong>Water:</strong> Carry full bottles from Stockade Ridge (or top off at any source on the approach). No water on the ridge.</li>
</ul>
<h4 id="tips">Tips</h4>
<ul>
<li><strong>Start early.</strong> The Tooth of Time summit should be reached before noon to avoid afternoon lightning exposure. Leave Stockade Ridge by or before 7 a.m.</li>
<li>The final Class 3 section requires clear thinking — do not rush inexperienced crew members on the boulders. Move steadily, use three points of contact, and help each other.</li>
<li>Pack drop at the summit base: designate one or two crew members to stay with packs while the others summit if pack security is a concern (it rarely is at Philmont, but plan for it).</li>
<li>Descend carefully — tired legs after 11 days on trail plus emotional peak energy at the summit can lead to careless footing on the descent.</li>
<li>Build in extra time — the Awards Campfire has a scheduled start; confirm the time with Base Camp staff on arrival and plan accordingly.</li>
<li>This is a fitting end: the crew that started by looking up at the Tooth from Base Camp finishes by standing on top of it.</li>
</ul>
