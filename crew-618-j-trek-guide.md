## Logistics and Acclimatization Timeline

Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS) is a significant risk for crews arriving from lower elevations. Our crew follows a structured acclimatization schedule before stepping onto the trail.

* **June 16, 2026:** Depart Raleigh-Durham International Airport (RDU) on United flight UA 2270 to Denver, Colorado. Upon arrival, load gear into two reserved minivans and drive south to Colorado Springs.
* **June 16–17, 2026:** Two-night acclimatization layover at the Great Wolf Lodge, Colorado Springs (~6,035 ft). Active acclimatization includes the Garden of the Gods and the Manitou Incline.
* **June 18, 2026:** Drive the final stretch to Cimarron, NM. Arrive for check-in at Philmont Base Camp (Day 1).
* **June 30, 2026:** Return to Denver and fly home to RDU on United flight UA 2671.

## Executive Summary

This is a 12-day South Country loop — Philmont's classic southern circuit. The route follows Rayado Creek up to Fish Camp, swings west through Apache Springs territory, climbs to the South Country's hub at Beaubien for a two-night layover, pushes north to Black Mountain for shooting sports and rock climbing at Miners Park, then arcs back southeast via Bear Caves and Urraca Mesa before finishing with the iconic Tooth of Time summit on the final day.

Key characteristics of this itinerary:
- **Primarily south country terrain:** canyon corridors and open meadows below 9,000 ft for most of the trek — more forgiving on altitude acclimatization than north country routes
- **Two major layover camps:** Beaubien (2 nights with showers and cowboy programming) and Miners Park (rock climbing day)
- **Diverse program portfolio:** every major program category is represented — living history, shooting sports, equestrian, challenge/ropes, archery, logging heritage, and technical climbing
- **One dry camp on Day 11** at Stockade Ridge — classified dry (no developed water system) but a natural water source exists at camp per the official itinerary
- **Fitness note:** The approach to Black Mountain on Day 8 (via the south-face trail of Black Mountain) is rated among the most difficult single-day ascents on the ranch; prepare the crew mentally for it
- **Final push:** Day 12 is emotionally high but physically demanding — Tooth of Time is a Class 3 scramble after a full morning of hiking

## Day-by-Day Camp Briefings

### Day 1 — Camping HQ (Base Camp)
**Elevation:** ~6,720 ft
**Type:** Staffed (full services)
**Program:** Opening Campfire
**Coordinates:** 36.453513, -104.962480 | UTM 13S 503362E 4034252N

#### Overview
Arrival day. Crews check in, receive initial orientation, meet their assigned Philmont Ranger (who will accompany you through Day 3 or 4 of the trek), sort gear, and attend the Opening Campfire. Base Camp sits in the broad Cimarron valley with the Tooth of Time visible on the southern skyline — crews will return over that ridge on Day 12.

#### Programs & What to Expect
The **Opening Campfire** is a Philmont tradition — a large evening gathering with storytelling, singing, and an inspirational send-off. It sets the tone for the trek and is genuinely memorable. Attendance is expected for all crew members.

The Ranger will conduct an initial gear check briefing and introduce themselves. Use this evening to finalize crew gear, confirm cooking assignments, and ensure all medications are accounted for.

#### Gear/Clothing Notes
- This is the last night with full amenities (hot showers, flush toilets, dining hall) for most of the trip
- Shower tonight; do laundry if needed
- Resupply food from the commissary if anything was forgotten
- Confirm crew bear bag and bear canister protocols with the Ranger

#### Tips
- Get to bed early — Day 2 starts with a Ranger shakedown and a bus ride to the trailhead in the afternoon
- Review the itinerary map with the crew so everyone understands the route
- Altitude: Base Camp is at ~6,720 ft; some crew members from lower elevations may feel mild symptoms. Hydrate well

### Day 2 — Olympia
**Elevation:** ~6,731 ft
**Type:** Trail camp (no staff)
**Program:** Ranger Training; Trail Camp
**Start (Rayado Trailhead):** 36.365230, -104.930362 | UTM 13S 506247E 4024461N
**Camp (Olympia):** 36.371734, -104.968928 | UTM 13S 502787E 4025181N

#### Overview
The crew boards a bus in the afternoon (typically around 2:30 p.m.) to the **Rayado Trailhead** — the southern entry point into Philmont's backcountry. This is a short first hiking day by design, giving the Ranger time to conduct training before camp. Olympia sits at nearly the same elevation as Base Camp, tucked in the Rayado Creek drainage.

#### Programs & What to Expect
The **Ranger shakedown** happens at the trailhead. The Ranger will check every pack, pull unnecessary weight, and demonstrate proper fitting. This is not optional and can result in gear being left at the trailhead — trust the process. Common cuts include extra clothes, heavy footwear substitutions, and oversized gear.

**Ranger training** continues through the afternoon hike and into camp — Leave No Trace, proper food storage, campsite selection, bear bag hanging, water purification, and stove operation. The Ranger will evaluate the crew and may extend training into Day 3.

**Rayado Rancho / Kit Carson Museum (Passthrough):** The official itinerary notes a passthrough program at Rayado on Day 2 — the bus route passes through the historic Rayado settlement, where Philmont staff interpret the 1848 Kit Carson and Lucien Maxwell era. The Kit Carson Museum is here. This is a brief opportunity on the way to the trailhead; confirm availability with the Ranger.

The hike to Olympia is short (2.6 miles per official itinerary) but may involve **water crossings** of Rayado Creek, especially early in the season when snowmelt can make the creek run fast. Do not underestimate these crossings with full packs.

#### Gear/Clothing Notes
- Trekking poles are especially useful at water crossings — keep them accessible in your pack's side pocket for Day 2
- Gaiters are helpful if the creek is running high
- Your feet will likely get wet at crossings; have dry camp socks accessible (not buried in the bottom of the pack)

#### Tips
- The Ranger will set expectations for the whole crew — engage positively; they know the mountain
- Hang bear bags tonight even though it is your first night; develop the habit immediately
- First night at altitude for some crew members; watch for headaches and encourage extra water intake

### Day 3 — Abreu *(showers available)*
**Elevation:** ~7,350 ft (approximate; Abreu sits in the Rayado drainage above Olympia)
**Type:** Staffed camp
**Program:** Cantina, Lodge Tours, Mexican Homestead / Dinner
**Start (Olympia):** 36.371734, -104.968928 | UTM 13S 502787E 4025181N
**Camp (Abreu):** 36.379133, -105.015701 | UTM 13S 498592E 4026001N

#### Overview
Abreu is one of Philmont's most distinctive and beloved camps — a living history homestead set in **1912 New Mexico**, where staff portray the Abreu family who purchased this ranch from Lucien Maxwell. The family has deep roots in northern New Mexico dating to the 1700s. The setting is a working-style homestead with adobe structures, animals, and period-appropriate activities.

#### Programs & What to Expect

**Mexican Homestead Program:** Staff in period dress interpret daily life on a New Mexican homestead. Confirmed programs for Itinerary 12-1 at Abreu: goat keeping, chicken tending, animal husbandry, adobe brick-making, fiber arts, food/cooking demonstrations, and self-guided fishing on the Rayado. Scouts can participate hands-on. This is an immersive living history experience, not a lecture.

**Cantina:** The cantina is a highlight that crews talk about for years. An adobe building with a courtyard and grape arbor, it sells cold root beer, peanuts, maps, and basic supplies. Sitting in actual chairs after two days of hiking is, as one Philmont article put it, "a significant and highly uncommon luxury." The staffer running the cantina also offers games. Budget a half hour here — it is worth it.

**Lodge Tours / Cabin Tours:** Staff walk crews through the historic Abreu homestead buildings, explaining construction techniques and family history.

**Mexican Dinner:** An evening meal prepared by the Abreu staff — a full New Mexican-style dinner served to the crew. This is a staffed dinner, not crew cooking. One of the most anticipated meals of any South Country trek. Note: crews with dietary restrictions should alert Philmont in advance during pre-trek registration.

#### Gear/Clothing Notes
- No special gear required
- Showers may be available — confirm with the Ranger; if so, use them
- Bring a little cash or a debit card if the cantina accepts card (cash is safer; many backcountry cantinas are cash only)

#### Tips
- Arrive with enough time before dinner to do the homestead program and cantina; the dinner is typically in the evening
- The Ranger may still be with the crew through Day 3 — this is often the farewell day for the Ranger, so use the morning for any remaining questions before they leave
- The camp is named for the Abreu family; staff portray real historical figures — engage with them in character for a better experience
- **Advisor Coffee** is available at Abreu.

### Day 4 — Fish Camp
**Elevation:** ~7,040 ft (junction of Rayado Creek and Agua Fria Creek)
**Type:** Trail camp — No services (per 2026 official itinerary)
**Program:** Trail Camp - No services
**Start (Abreu):** 36.379133, -105.015701 | UTM 13S 498592E 4026001N
**Camp (Fish Camp):** 36.387820, -105.102720 | UTM 13S 490788E 4026970N

#### Overview
Fish Camp occupies the site of Waite Phillips's original hunting and fishing lodge at the confluence of the Rayado and Agua Fria creeks. The setting is creek-side with tall cottonwoods and willows — one of the more lush, shaded camps in the south country.

**For Crew 618-J in 2026, the official Philmont Adventure Itinerary (page 25 of the Itinerary Guidebook) explicitly lists Day 4 as "Trail Camp - No services."** There are no staff-run programs assigned to this stop. The crew overnights here and moves on.

#### Historical Programs (Not Available This Crew)
Fish Camp has in prior years offered staff-run programs including fly tying, fly fishing, and tours of the original Phillips hunting lodge (which includes a hide from what is said to be the last grizzly bear shot in Colfax County, NM). These programs are not scheduled for Itinerary 12-1 in 2026. If staff happen to be present for maintenance or other purposes, programs may be offered on a standby basis — but do not count on it.

**Self-guided fishing:** The official itinerary includes a general note that "itinerary may provide opportunities for fishing with personal equipment. New Mexico Fishing License is required." Self-guided fishing with personal equipment in the Rayado is permitted here, provided crew members have valid licenses (see Gear/Clothing Notes below).

#### Gear/Clothing Notes
- No special gear required
- Creek-side terrain; gaiters or water-resistant footwear helpful if weather has been wet
- **Fishing licenses** (source: philmontscoutranch.org/resources/preparing-for-philmont/fishing-license/):
- **Youth 11 & under:** No license required.
- **Youth 12–17:** $2.00 Philmont Youth 10-Day License (cash, exact change). Available at Abreu, Fish Camp, Hunting Lodge, Phillips Junction, or Tooth of Time Traders. Alternatively, a Youth Annual License can be purchased online in advance ($9 NM resident / $19 non-resident).
- **Adults 18+:** A New Mexico fishing license is required and **must be purchased before entering the backcountry** — licenses are not available once you are on trail. Purchase online at onlinesales.wildlife.state.nm.us or at the Tooth of Time Traders on Day 1. A $2.00 vendor fee is added to all adult purchases. Check the NM Dept. of Game & Fish site for current 2026 license prices before the trip.

#### Tips
- This is a lower-elevation, water-adjacent camp; mosquitoes can be active, especially at dusk — bring insect repellent
- Water is available from a purified spigot; top off here
- Enjoy the shade; Bonita Canyon and the higher country coming up will be more exposed

### Day 5 — Buck Creek
**Elevation:** ~9,131 ft
**Type:** Trail camp (no staff at Buck Creek itself)
**Program:** Archery (3D) at Apache Springs, Commissary at Apache Springs, Jicarilla Apache Life at Apache Springs; Trail Camp at Buck Creek
**Start (Fish Camp):** 36.387820, -105.102720 | UTM 13S 490788E 4026970N
**Passthrough (Apache Springs):** 36.387479, -105.144133 | UTM 13S 487074E 4026937N
**Camp (Buck Creek):** 36.411196, -105.130419 | UTM 13S 488307E 4029566N

#### Overview
Day 5 involves a significant elevation gain — from Fish Camp (~7,040 ft) up to Buck Creek at over 9,100 ft. The route passes through **Apache Springs** (a staffed camp at approximately 9,400 ft), where the day's programs occur, before descending slightly to the Buck Creek trail camp for the night. This is one of the longer and more demanding hiking days on the itinerary.

#### Programs & What to Expect (at Apache Springs)

**3D Archery — Jicarilla Apache Style:** Apache Springs is home to Philmont's only backcountry archery course. The course uses 3D foam animal targets positioned throughout the terrain — deer, elk, bear, and other animals in realistic poses at varying distances. Equipment (bows, arrows, arm guards) is provided by the camp. No prior archery experience is required. Staff explain the cultural context of archery within Jicarilla Apache tradition.

**Jicarilla Apache Life Program:** Apache Springs is Philmont's primary interpretive camp for the Jicarilla Apache people who inhabited this region until the mid-19th century. The program includes:
- Tipi tours (the camp features a village of historically accurate tipis with genuine artifacts)
- Flint knapping — Scouts make real arrowheads using traditional stone-knapping techniques; this is a genuine skill and takes focused effort
- Sweat lodge — a cultural and spiritual experience conducted respectfully
- History talks on Jicarilla history, lifestyle, and displacement
- Optional sunset meadow hike

**Commissary:** Apache Springs operates a full commissary with food resupply and white gas. It also has a small trading post where basic gear can be purchased. This is a good place to resupply any food that has been consumed faster than planned.

#### Gear/Clothing Notes
- No special archery gear needed — all equipment is provided at Apache Springs
- Closed-toed shoes are required for archery (already standard Philmont footwear)
- The approach to Apache Springs involves significant climbing; trekking poles are useful
- Buck Creek at 9,131 ft can be cold overnight — layers ready for camp

#### Tips
- Apache Springs sits at the highest point of the day (9,400 ft); crew members may feel altitude effects even if acclimatized — keep the pace moderate on the climb
- The flint knapping activity takes time; if the crew wants to complete it, do not rush through the other programs
- Top off water at Apache Springs commissary before the descent to Buck Creek
- Buck Creek is a trail camp with no staff; bears are active in this area — hang bags properly
- The commissary sometimes stocks fresh fruit; take advantage of it
- **Advisor Coffee** is available at Apache Springs.

#### Trail Navigation Data
*Supplementary trail data from PhilTrek.com. Use alongside official mileage for in-field timing — do not substitute for official figures.*

The official 7.5-mile day splits into two measurable segments at Apache Springs:

| Segment | Distance | % of Day |
|---------|-------------|----------|
| Fish Camp → Apache Springs | ~3.4 mi | ~46% |
| Apache Springs → Buck Creek | ~3.9 mi | ~54% |

Apache Springs falls almost exactly at the midpoint of the day's hiking. The climb continues beyond Apache Springs — the second half is the more demanding half. If the crew is running behind schedule at Apache Springs, they still have more than half the day's mileage remaining before Buck Creek. Budget program time at Apache Springs accordingly.

### Day 6 — Beaubien *(showers available)*
**Elevation:** ~7,920 ft
**Type:** Staffed camp (largest in the South Country; 32 campsites)
**Program:** Campfire (evening), Chuck Wagon Dinner, Horse Rides, Western Lore
**Start (Buck Creek):** 36.411196, -105.130419 | UTM 13S 488307E 4029566N
**Camp (Beaubien):** 36.423576, -105.103246 | UTM 13S 490745E 4030936N

#### Overview
Beaubien is the undisputed hub of the South Country — the largest staffed camp at Philmont with 32 campsites spread across one of the ranch's biggest meadows. Set in the period of **1875 high mountain ranching**, the camp brings to life the cowboy era with horses, branding irons, roping, and period dress. The massive meadow, framed by ridgelines on three sides, offers sweeping views and star-filled skies. Virtually every South Country itinerary passes through Beaubien, and for good reason.

This crew stays two nights (Days 6 and 7 — the layover), making Beaubien the physical and morale anchor of the trek.

#### Programs & What to Expect

**Horse Rides:** The camp offers guided trail rides on horseback. This is a genuine horseback excursion, not a brief loop — riders follow a trail through the meadow and surrounding terrain with Beaubien staff as guides. The historical theme is 1875 ranch life. **Horses are limited and require reservation through Logistics during Day 1 Base Camp check-in** — the Lead Advisor must arrange this before departing the trailhead. Slot times may fill quickly.

**Western Lore / Branding:** Staff teach roping techniques, and crews have the opportunity to brand leather goods — hiking boots, belts, and other brandable items — using the Philmont brands (Slash Crazy S and/or Bar P Crazy S). This is a popular and lasting souvenir.

**Chuck Wagon Dinner:** A staff-prepared cowboy dinner served from a chuck wagon — a full meal that is one of the highlights of the South Country experience. Enjoy it.

**Evening Campfire:** Beaubien's campfire program is a full western show with storytelling, cowboy poetry or songs, and humor. It is performed by the Beaubien staff in character.

**Purified water** is available on-site. **Showers** are available at Beaubien — a major morale item after several days on the trail.

#### Gear/Clothing Notes

**For Horse Rides:**
- **Long pants are required** — no shorts or bare skin on the legs
- **Closed-toed shoes are required** — hiking boots (which everyone should already have) are ideal
- Western boots are not necessary; trail runners or hiking boots are fine
- Height and weight restrictions apply; Philmont publishes a chart — participants over the limit cannot ride. Advisors should confirm weight eligibility during pre-trek paperwork
- Secure loose items before mounting; camera straps and dangling accessories can spook horses

**For Branding:**
- Have your boot, belt, or other leather item accessible if you want it branded

#### Tips
- Showers: confirm hours with staff; they are often limited to certain windows. Get the crew there early
- The chuck wagon dinner is not crew-cooked; enjoy the break from stove duty
- The nearby Phillips Junction commissary is approximately 2 trail miles to the southwest and has fresh fruit, a trading post, and additional showers — worth the detour if the crew needs supplies
- Take a slow walk around the meadow at dusk; the Beaubien meadow at sunset is one of Philmont's signature views
- **Advisor Coffee** is available at Beaubien.

### Day 7 — Beaubien (Layover Day) *(showers available)*
**Elevation:** ~7,920 ft
**Type:** Staffed camp (same camp, second night)
**Program:** Layover day
**Coordinates (Beaubien):** 36.423576, -105.103246 | UTM 13S 490745E 4030936N

#### Overview
The layover day at Beaubien is a full rest day — no mandatory mileage, no breaking camp at dawn. This is intentional in the itinerary design; by Day 7 the crew has been on trail for 5 full days and the harder terrain (Black Mountain, Bear Caves, Urraca, Tooth of Time) is all ahead. Use this day deliberately.

#### Programs & What to Expect

All Beaubien programs remain available on the layover day — any programs not completed on Day 6 (horse rides, branding, roping) can be done today.

**Crew options for layover day:**
- **Conservation project — 7:30am start:** The layover day begins with the scheduled forest fuel reduction project at Beaubien, facilitated by Philmont Conservation Department staff. The work: removing trees that have encroached into the meadow over the last 100 years and building slash piles to return the forest to a more fire-resilient state. This 3-hour project counts toward the Arrowhead Award requirement and toward the 50-Miler Award conservation hours. Due to multi-season progress, the active worksite may be a mile or more from camp — plan for a walk to the site.
- Repeat or complete any Beaubien programs
- Day hike to Black Mountain (Day 8's destination) to scout the approach — this is a legitimate fitness option for stronger hikers and gives a preview of tomorrow's demanding trail
- Rest and gear maintenance — this is the right choice for most of the crew
- Phillips Junction commissary run if resupply is needed
- Gear repair, blister care, sock drying

#### Gear/Clothing Notes
- Same as Day 6 for horse rides
- This is the last shower opportunity until Miners Park (Day 8, which does have showers); use it

#### Tips
- **The scoutmaster recommendation:** split the day between active program and genuine rest. Do not schedule the crew so tightly that the layover day becomes another full-activity day
- Blister management is critical today — address foot issues proactively before the Black Mountain climb on Day 8
- Have the crew do a gear inventory and repack; Days 8–12 are the most demanding stretch
- The climb to Black Mountain (Day 8) is reputedly one of the hardest single-day hikes on Philmont — brief the crew on Day 7 evening so they go to bed mentally prepared
- If any crew member is showing injury or illness signs, this is the day to assess and make contingency plans with staff
- **Advisor Coffee** is available at Beaubien (both nights).

### Day 8 — Miners Park *(showers available)*
**Elevation of Miners Park:** ~8,002 ft
**Programs at Black Mountain (sub-camp):** Black Powder Rifle, Blacksmithing, Post Civil War Settlers
**Programs at Miners Park:** Rock Climbing, Commissary
**Type:** Staffed camp (Miners Park is staffed; Black Mountain is a remote staffed sub-camp)
**Start (Beaubien):** 36.423576, -105.103246 | UTM 13S 490745E 4030936N
**Passthrough (Black Mountain):** 36.442549, -105.093307 | UTM 13S 491638E 4033040N
**Camp (Miners Park):** 36.423674, -105.041596 | UTM 13S 496271E 4030943N

#### Overview
Day 8 is the most demanding hiking day on this itinerary. The crew leaves Beaubien and climbs to **Black Mountain**, one of only two Philmont staffed camps not accessible by road (all supplies go in and out by burro). The Black Mountain south-face trail has been described as among the three most difficult hikes in New Mexico. After completing Black Mountain's programs, the crew descends and hikes to Miners Park for rock climbing and the night camp (which does have showers and a commissary).

This is a long day in terms of total mileage and elevation change. Start early.

#### Programs & What to Expect

**Black Mountain — Post Civil War Settlers / Colfax County War (1865–1890 period):**
Staff at Black Mountain portray settlers of the post-Civil War era. The specific historical theme for Itinerary 12-1 is the **Colfax County War** — the violent land-grant dispute of the 1870s–1880s between Maxwell Land Grant Company interests and homesteaders who refused to leave. This conflict drew gunfighters, federal troops, and eventually resulted in significant loss of life in the very landscape the crew is hiking through. The main program building is the original Black Mountain cabin, constructed in the 1890s by brothers prospecting for gold in the North Fork Urraca drainage.

**Gold Panning:** Confirmed for Itinerary 12-1 at Black Mountain. Staff demonstrate panning technique in the creek; Scouts can try to find color in the gravel. Gold was genuinely mined in this drainage.

**Black Powder Rifle Shooting:**
Crews fire muzzle-loading black powder rifles at targets on a dedicated range. Staff demonstrate safe loading procedures (powder measure, patch, ball, ramrod), proper stance, and firing. No prior firearms experience is required — in fact, the muzzle-loading process is so different from modern firearms that prior experience is largely irrelevant. Scouts load and fire under close supervision. This is one of Philmont's most popular programs and a program that no Ranger or crew who has done it forgets.

No outside firearms, ammunition, or black powder is permitted on the ranch. All equipment is provided. BSA Youth Protection and firearms safety protocols are strictly observed.

**Blacksmithing:**
Crews work with a forge and anvil to create a simple iron object — typically a hook or spike — under staff instruction. No experience needed. This is physically engaging, historically interesting, and produces a tangible take-home item.

**Miners Park — Rock Climbing:**
Miners Park is one of Philmont's three dedicated climbing camps. The program area is a 20–30 minute hike from the main campsite, past a meadow where mountain lions have been observed hunting. The principal climbing feature is a formation called **Betty's Bra**, a natural rock face with established routes of varying difficulty. There is also a climbing wall, bouldering area, rappel routes, and crate stacking.

**All climbing equipment is provided:** harnesses, helmets, and belay systems. Personal climbing harnesses and helmets are not permitted (liability standardization). Personal climbing shoes are allowed and welcome if crew members have them. For those without personal climbing shoes, standard hiking boots or trail runners work adequately on most routes.

**Footwear requirement:** closed-toed shoes are mandatory. Sandals and open-toed footwear are not permitted at the climbing area.

**Commissary:** Miners Park has a commissary for food resupply.

#### Gear/Clothing Notes

**For Rock Climbing:**
- Harnesses and helmets: provided by Philmont — no need to bring your own
- Personal climbing shoes: allowed and beneficial if crew members own them; not required
- Closed-toed shoes minimum requirement
- Avoid baggy shorts or pants that can bunch in a harness — fitted pants or shorts that can be tucked are better
- Remove keys, large belt buckles, or items that will dig into you in a harness

**For Black Powder Rifle:**
- No special preparation or gear needed
- Eye protection is typically provided or required by the range; confirm with staff
- Hearing protection may be provided; if you have lightweight foam earplugs, bring them

**For Black Mountain Climb:**
- Trekking poles are strongly recommended for the descent, which is steep
- Extra water — the south-face approach is exposed and demanding; carry at least 2 liters from Beaubien
- Start the day early to allow time for all programs and the hike to Miners Park

#### Tips
- Black Mountain's elevation is above Miners Park; the crew will descend to Miners Park after the Black Mountain programs
- The day is long — brief the crew the night before (during Day 7 layover) on the early start time
- Showers at Miners Park: take them, this is the last opportunity for several days
- Miners Park campsites are in a broad meadow setting; the rock formation area is a separate hike from camp
- **Advisor Coffee** is available at both Black Mountain (passthrough) and Miners Park.

### Day 9 — Bear Caves
**Elevation:** ~8,423 ft
**Type:** Trail camp (no staff at Bear Caves itself)
**Program:** Campfire (evening) at Crater Lake; Continental Tie and Lumber Company at Crater Lake; Trail Camp at Bear Caves
**Start (Miners Park):** 36.423674, -105.041596 | UTM 13S 496271E 4030943N
**Passthrough (Crater Lake):** 36.405565, -105.048942 | UTM 13S 495612E 4028934N
**Camp (Bear Caves):** 36.404802, -105.033858 | UTM 13S 496964E 4028849N

#### Overview
Bear Caves is a trail camp in the southwest country, positioned near the Crater Lake staffed camp area where the day's programs occur. The name refers to actual rock overhangs and shallow caves in the area that have historically attracted bear activity — proper food storage is especially important here. The terrain transitions from the Black Mountain area toward the rolling country approaching Urraca Mesa.

#### Programs & What to Expect (at Crater Lake)

**Continental Tie and Lumber Company Program:**
Crater Lake's program brings to life the early 20th-century logging operations of the Continental Tie and Lumber Company (CT&L), which operated in Philmont's canyon country in the 1900s–1910s. CT&L cut timber in the Ponil and Rayado drainages to produce railroad ties for the expanding rail network.

Activities include:
- **Spar pole climbing:** Scouts use climbing spurs (gaffs) and a safety belt to ascend a tall wooden spar pole — a traditional logging skill. This is a physically demanding upper-body and grip challenge. No prior experience needed; staff teach the technique.
- **Crosscut saw:** Two-person traditional saws used to cut logs — "misery whip" in logger slang. Crews work in pairs. Pure teamwork.
- **Hand-hewn railroad tie construction:** Using a broadaxe, scouts learn to shape a round log into a flat-sided railroad tie.
- **Loggerball:** A logger-themed variant of baseball, played in the evening

**Evening Campfire at Crater Lake:** The Crater Lake campfire features a magnificent view of the Tooth of Time on the southeastern skyline — the crew's Day 12 destination is visible from here, which is motivating.

#### Gear/Clothing Notes
- Long pants are recommended (and may be required) for spar pole climbing, which involves leg gaffs and a tree trunk; long pants protect against bark abrasion
- Sturdy footwear (hiking boots) for spar pole work
- No special gear beyond standard trail kit

#### Tips
- Bear activity is historically noted in this area; hang food bags and dispose of waste properly
- The spar pole climb is physically demanding on grip and arms; crew members with upper body strength concerns should still attempt it — staff will help them get as high as they can safely go
- Crater Lake's view of the Tooth of Time is worth noting for the crew: that ridge is where they finish in 3 days
- **Advisor Coffee** is available at Crater Lake (passthrough).

#### Trail Navigation Data
*Supplementary trail data from PhilTrek.com. Use alongside official mileage for in-field timing — do not substitute for official figures.*

The official 3.1-mile day splits into two segments at Crater Lake:

| Segment | Distance | % of Day |
|---------|-------------|----------|
| Miners Park → Crater Lake | ~1.8 mi | ~64% |
| Crater Lake → Bear Caves | ~1.0 mi | ~36% |

Nearly two-thirds of the day's hiking is complete before the crew reaches Crater Lake programs. Arrival at programs will be late morning to midday — not an early-morning stop. ### Day 10 — Urraca
**Elevation:** ~7,968 ft
**Type:** Staffed camp
**Program:** Campfire (evening), Challenge Events (COPE)
**Start (Bear Caves):** 36.404802, -105.033858 | UTM 13S 496964E 4028849N
**Camp (Urraca):** 36.408988, -104.990507 | UTM 13S 500851E 4029313N

#### Overview
Urraca sits on the north side of Urraca Mesa at the edge of a broad meadow in ponderosa pines, with views north toward Base Camp. The approach to Urraca involves a climb up and over the mesa — the trail winds through pinon-dotted terrain with sustained elevation gain and limited shade. Urraca Mesa reaches 8,594 ft at its highest point; the camp is just below that on the north slope.

Urraca is one of Philmont's challenge event camps. The program focus is teamwork, leadership, and individual growth through group initiative challenges rather than technical skills.

**Philmont lore:** Urraca Mesa has a campfire legend attached to it — stories of mysterious blue lights and unexplained occurrences on the mesa. The staff lean into this atmosphere. Cattle from surrounding grazing allotments sometimes wander through camp; this is normal and expected.

#### Programs & What to Expect

**Challenge Events (COPE-style):**
Unlike the high-ropes COPE at RMSC (Day 11), Urraca's challenge events are ground-level or low-element initiatives — group problem-solving challenges that require the entire crew to work together. Programs at Urraca have historically referenced the "Dan Beard" or "Head of Dean" challenge course design. Expect:
- Initiative games (e.g., group standing on a shrinking platform)
- Trust events
- Problem-solving scenarios that have no obvious single solution
- Debrief discussions about leadership and group dynamics

The goal is explicit: crews arriving late in a trek have had days to develop interpersonal dynamics — tensions, friendships, roles. The COPE program forces crews to reflect on those dynamics intentionally.

**Evening Campfire:** Urraca's campfire program is atmospheric, particularly given the mesa setting and the staff's use of local legend.

#### Gear/Clothing Notes
- No special gear needed for ground-level challenge events
- Comfortable, flexible clothing (no loose jewelry that can catch) for initiative games
- Trekking poles not needed during challenge events; leave them at camp

#### Tips
- The hike to Urraca involves real climbing; despite being mid-route, the crew will feel the accumulated miles — set a steady pace
- Crews may encounter cattle on or near the trail approaching the mesa; give them space and do not chase or corner them
- At 7,968 ft, nights can be cold; have sleeping bag liners or warm layers accessible
- The evening campfire with the mesa legend is one of the more atmospheric Philmont experiences — encourage crew members to stay present rather than collapsing into tents immediately after dinner
- Inspiration Point is a notable short side hike from Urraca offering broader views; confirm with staff if accessible
- **Advisor Coffee** is available at Urraca.

### Day 11 — Stockade Ridge *(dry camp — no water at camp)*
**Elevation:** Approximately 7,200–7,500 ft (just above Philmont Reservoir, at the base of Tooth of Time ridge)
**Type:** Trail camp (no staff)
**Program:** COPE at RMSC; Trail Camp
**Start (Urraca):** 36.408988, -104.990507 | UTM 13S 500851E 4029313N
**Passthrough (RMSC):** 36.434049, -104.990120 | UTM 13S 500886E 4032093N
**Camp (Stockade Ridge):** 36.437662, -105.007633 | UTM 13S 499316E 4032493N

#### Overview
Stockade Ridge is a newer trail camp (established 2013) situated just above Philmont Reservoir at the base of Tooth of Time Ridge — the crew's final approach terrain for Day 12. The camp offers dramatic direct views of the Tooth of Time above. **The itinerary designates this as a dry camp**, meaning no water source is available at the campsite itself.

**Rocky Mountain Scout Camp (RMSC)** is located approximately 1 mile east of Stockade Ridge. RMSC is primarily used for NAYLE (National Advanced Youth Leadership Experience) training, but some trek itineraries route crews through RMSC for a COPE (Challenging Outdoor Personal Experience) high-ropes program. Note: RMSC was historically closed to overnight camping due to dam/reservoir proximity regulations; the COPE program there is a day-use visit while the crew camps at Stockade Ridge.

#### Programs & What to Expect

**High COPE at RMSC:**
This is a high-element ropes course — distinct from the ground-level challenge events at Urraca. High COPE involves elements elevated above the ground with harnesses and belay systems:
- High ropes traverses (cables and bridges between elevated platforms)
- Climbing tower
- Zip line or rappel elements (varies by year and staffing)
- Initiative games at the low COPE level as warm-up

The program builds on the teamwork developed throughout the trek — by Day 11, crews have 10 days of shared experience to draw on. All safety equipment (harnesses, helmets, belay devices) is provided. Participants must follow BSA height/weight guidelines for elevated elements. Instructors are certified COPE facilitators.

**Closed-toed shoes are required.** No dangling jewelry or accessories that can catch in harness hardware.

#### Gear/Clothing Notes

**For Dry Camp (updated):**
- The official 2026 itinerary notes "Water @ Stockade" — a natural water source exists at the campsite itself. However, the camp remains classified as a dry camp (no developed/piped system), meaning the source may be limited, seasonal, or require a short walk. **Treat this as a dry camp and arrive with full bottles.** Do not count on the on-site source until you can confirm it with staff on Day 10 at Urraca.
- Plan to carry a minimum of 2–3 liters per person from the last confirmed source on the approach trail
- Purification tablets or filter mandatory — any natural source must be treated

**For COPE:**
- Harnesses and helmets provided — no personal gear needed
- Wear fitted clothing (not loose/baggy) that will not bunch in a harness
- Closed-toed shoes required (hiking boots, standard equipment)
- Leave trekking poles at the campsite for the COPE visit

#### Tips
- Water management is the key challenge of Day 11 — brief the crew specifically on how much water to carry and where the last fill point is on the approach trail
- Stockade Ridge has outstanding direct views of the Tooth of Time from below — use this evening to mentally preview tomorrow's climb
- Day 12 starts early (the crew needs to climb the Tooth, do the awards campfire at Base Camp, and pack out); get to bed on time
- Despite the dry camp designation, the nearby Philmont Reservoir is visible — it is a managed water source and NOT safe to use untreated (dam and reservoir contamination risk)
- The evening before the Tooth of Time climb is a natural moment for a crew reflection — consider a brief informal roundtable about the trek before lights out

#### Trail Navigation Data
*Supplementary trail data from PhilTrek.com. The official 4.7 mi includes the RMSC detour; the direct Urraca-to-Stockade Ridge trail is shorter.*

| Segment | Distance | Notes |
|---------|----------|-------|
| Urraca → RMSC junction | ~2.7 mi | Along main trail toward Stockade Ridge |
| RMSC detour (junction ↔ RMSC) | ~0.5 mi each way | Off main trail |
| RMSC → Stockade Ridge | ~0.9 mi | After returning to main trail |
| **Total with RMSC detour** | **~4.7 mi** | **Matches official** |

The COPE program at RMSC occurs roughly 75% through the main trail distance (2.7 of 3.6 direct miles), with approximately 0.9 miles of main trail still remaining after the crew returns from RMSC. Do not let COPE run so late that the final push to Stockade Ridge dry camp becomes a race against darkness — water management there requires arriving with a clear head and daylight.

### Day 12 — Camping HQ (Return)
**Elevation change:** From Stockade Ridge (~7,200–7,500 ft) up to Tooth of Time summit (9,003 ft) then descend to Base Camp (~6,720 ft)
**Type:** Return to staffed Base Camp
**Program:** Climb Tooth of Time; Hike in via Tooth Ridge; Awards Campfire
**Start (Stockade Ridge):** 36.437662, -105.007633 | UTM 13S 499316E 4032493N
**Finish (Camping HQ):** 36.453513, -104.962480 | UTM 13S 503362E 4034252N

#### Overview
Day 12 is the emotional and physical finale of the trek. The crew climbs the Tooth of Time Ridge, summits the iconic Tooth (9,003 ft), and descends into Base Camp for the Awards Campfire. The Tooth of Time is Philmont's signature landmark — a dramatic rock spire visible from Base Camp and from miles around. Summiting it on the final day is a rite of passage.

The approach follows Tooth Ridge from Stockade Ridge, traversing a rocky ridgeline before the final Class 3 scramble to the summit.

#### Programs & What to Expect

**Tooth Ridge Traverse:**
From Stockade Ridge, the trail climbs through ponderosa forest and then onto rocky, open ridgeline. Once above tree line, the terrain is large boulders and exposed rock. The approach along the ridge is Class 2 — rocky, requiring attention to footing, but not technical. Some sections involve navigating over and around large boulders.

From Shaefer's Pass (a saddle on the ridge), the trail to the Tooth is approximately 3.5 miles total to the summit. The final quarter mile to the top is the Class 3 section.

**Tooth of Time Summit — Class 3 Scramble:**
The final 440 feet of elevation gain to the summit is a genuine boulder scramble — Class 3 in the standard climbing classification, meaning hands and feet are used but no rope is needed. You will use your hands for balance and holds, particularly in the final approach. The scramble is manageable for any crew member who can hike Philmont; it is not technical climbing.

**Standard practice:** crews drop their full packs at the base of the final scramble and summit with day packs or just water bottles. Do not attempt the final scramble with a full 35–45 lb pack.

**At the summit:** 360-degree views of the Sangre de Cristo range, Cimarron valley, and surrounding Philmont terrain. The crew will receive a summit patch. This is a genuine high point of the BSA experience.

**Descent to Base Camp:** After summiting, descend via the established trail back to Base Camp. The trail is steep in places; trekking poles are useful on the descent.

**Awards Campfire:** Base Camp hosts an Awards Campfire for crews returning that day. Patches, recognitions, and program awards are distributed. The closing campfire marks the official end of the trek.

#### Gear/Clothing Notes

**For the Tooth of Time Scramble:**
- **Gloves:** Optional but recommended. The boulder field in the final scramble involves rough granite surfaces; thin leather work gloves or light hiking gloves protect palms during sustained scrambling. Not required, but experienced hikers bring them.
- **Sun protection is critical:** the ridge is fully exposed with minimal shade. Sunscreen (reapply), sunglasses, and a hat are essential. The scramble section can be hot in late morning/early afternoon.
- **Insect repellent:** The summit in warm season attracts large numbers of ladybugs and gnats. Descend even 10 feet below the summit to escape them.
- **Trekking poles:** Useful on the approach and descent. Stow them during the Class 3 scramble itself (poles are a hazard during hand-and-foot scrambling).
- **Rain awareness:** The Tooth is fully exposed to weather. If thunderstorms are building, do not be on or near the summit after noon. Check the forecast on Day 11 and be prepared to start early on Day 12 to summit before afternoon storm development.
- **Water:** Carry full bottles from Stockade Ridge (or top off at any source on the approach). No water on the ridge.

#### Tips
- **Start early.** The Tooth of Time summit should be reached before noon to avoid afternoon lightning exposure. Leave Stockade Ridge by or before 7 a.m.
- The final Class 3 section requires clear thinking — do not rush inexperienced crew members on the boulders. Move steadily, use three points of contact, and help each other.
- Pack drop at the summit base: designate one or two crew members to stay with packs while the others summit if pack security is a concern (it rarely is at Philmont, but plan for it).
- Descend carefully — tired legs after 11 days on trail plus emotional peak energy at the summit can lead to careless footing on the descent.
- Build in extra time — the Awards Campfire has a scheduled start; confirm the time with Base Camp staff on arrival and plan accordingly.
- This is a fitting end: the crew that started by looking up at the Tooth from Base Camp finishes by standing on top of it.

6. **Abreu shower availability:** Listed as a (s) shower camp in the itinerary. Confirm shower availability and hours; Abreu's showers are sometimes limited.

7. **COPE at Urraca vs. RMSC:** This itinerary includes challenge events at Urraca (Day 10, ground-level) and high COPE at RMSC (Day 11).
